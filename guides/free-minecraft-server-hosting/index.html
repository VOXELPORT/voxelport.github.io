Free Minecraft Server Hosting: No Sleep, No Queue (2026)

Free Minecraft hosting compared: Aternos, Minehut, Falix and your own PC with VoxelPort. What each free plan limits, and which to pick for your group.

“Free Minecraft hosting” usually means a cloud host that runs your server on their machines for free, with limits: the server sleeps when nobody is online, you may wait in a queue to start it, and RAM is capped. There is another free option: host on your own PC and let a relay put it online. Here is how they compare, and the honest catch with each.

Which one should you pick?

  • You have a PC that’s on while you play: host on it with VoxelPort. No sleep, no queue, your own RAM, no ads, and your world files stay on your PC.
  • Nobody in your group has a PC that can stay on: use a cloud host. Aternos is free forever; Minehut and Falix have free plans with paid upgrades.
  • You want a big modpack: free cloud plans struggle with heavy modpacks. Your own PC with 8 GB+ to spare, or a paid host, works much better.
  • You want it online 24/7 without a PC: that needs paid hosting; no free option does this well.

How free hosting on your own PC works

Normally, hosting at home means port forwarding, which doesn’t work on many connections (Jio, Airtel and most mobile data use CGNAT). VoxelPort avoids it: the app connects out to a VoxelPort relay, the relay gives your server a public address, and players join that address. Nothing on your router changes, and your home IP stays private.

VoxelPort has relays in India and in Europe, and the app picks the closer one, so friends near either get low ping. You get a free address like play.voxelport.in:26137, and supporters can claim a custom name like yourname.voxelport.in that friends join without a port number.

Set up a free server in 2 minutes

Download the Windows installer (the new app, 2.0 beta; Windows 10 and 11; if Windows warns, click More info → Run anyway; if Windows 11 Smart App Control blocks it, see the download page). It's free and open source; to host, join our Discord server once.

  1. Click + Add Server. Pick a template (Survival SMP, Creative, Modded…) or choose Vanilla, Paper or Fabric and a version.
  2. Choose how much RAM to give it, agree to the EULA, and click Install & start. VoxelPort downloads the right Java for you.
  3. Click Make public and copy your free address.
  4. Share it. Friends add it under Multiplayer → Add Server. Turn on Bedrock players in Settings so phone and console players can join too.
Moving from a cloud host? Download your world from it, put it in your new server’s folder as world, and start the server. See moving a world from Aternos.

The catch

Your PC has to be on while people play, and it uses your internet: each player needs roughly 0.1–0.5 Mbps of upload. Most home connections handle a group of friends easily. If that doesn’t suit your group, a free cloud host is the better choice, and that’s fine.

Questions

What is the best free Minecraft server hosting?

It depends on your PC. If you have a PC that can stay on while people play, hosting on it with VoxelPort is free with no sleep, no queue and no RAM cap. If you don’t, a cloud host like Aternos, Minehut or Falix runs the server for you, with limits on the free plan.

Is there free Minecraft hosting that doesn’t sleep?

Free cloud hosts usually stop or sleep the server when nobody is online, to save resources. A server on your own PC doesn’t: with VoxelPort it stays online until you stop it, as long as the PC is on.

Is VoxelPort really free?

Yes. The app, the relay and your public address are free and open source, with no ads and no card (hosts join our Discord server). You use your own PC’s power and internet. (Tips are optional; supporters get a badge, a pinned server and a custom yourname.voxelport.in address as a thank-you, and nothing free is ever taken away.)

How much RAM do free Minecraft hosts give?

Free plans are usually around 1–4 GB, depending on the host. On your own PC you choose: a PC with 16 GB of RAM can give a server 6–8 GB, enough for most modpacks.

Can Bedrock and phone players join a free server?

On VoxelPort, yes: turn on Bedrock players for a Paper or Fabric server and Java and Bedrock (phone, console, Windows) players join the same world. Several cloud hosts support this too, sometimes only on paid plans.
